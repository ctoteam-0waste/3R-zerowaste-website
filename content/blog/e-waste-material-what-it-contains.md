That old phone in your drawer, the broken laptop in the storeroom, the tangle of chargers nobody uses. They look like clutter, but they are **e waste material**, and what is inside them matters for your health, for the environment and for the resources we can recover.

This guide explains what e waste material is made of, which parts are valuable, which are hazardous, and what to do with it in India.

## Key takeaways

- **E waste material** is discarded electrical and electronic equipment, along with the metals, plastics, glass and chemicals inside it.
- It contains valuable materials such as copper, aluminium, iron, gold and silver, as well as hazardous ones such as lead, mercury and cadmium.
- Burning, dumping or dismantling e waste informally can release toxic substances. Use authorised recyclers instead.
- Businesses have legal responsibilities under India's e-waste rules; households can use doorstep pickups.

## What is e waste material?

E waste material is anything that comes out of electrical and electronic products once they are no longer used or wanted. That includes whole devices and also their components, such as circuit boards, batteries, cables, screens and casings.

It covers a wide range of items:

- **IT and telecom equipment:** laptops, desktops, monitors, phones, routers, printers
- **Household appliances:** refrigerators, washing machines, microwaves, air conditioners, fans
- **Consumer electronics:** televisions, speakers, cameras, headphones
- **Lighting:** CFLs, tube lights and LED lamps
- **Accessories:** chargers, cables, power banks, batteries

If it has a plug, a battery or a circuit board, it will become e waste one day. For a basic definition and household disposal tips, see our guide on [e waste in India and how to dispose of it](/blog/e-waste-india-disposal-guide).

## What materials are found in e waste?

Electronics are a mix of many materials, tightly bonded together. That is what makes them valuable to recover and difficult to handle safely.

| Material | Where it is found | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Iron and steel** | Frames, casings, appliance bodies | Easily recycled in large volumes |
| **Aluminium** | Heat sinks, casings, laptop bodies | Recycling uses far less energy than making new metal |
| **Copper** | Wires, cables, motors, circuit boards | Highly valuable and widely recovered |
| **Gold, silver, palladium** | Connectors and circuit boards (tiny amounts) | Precious metals recovered by specialised processes |
| **Plastics** | Casings, insulation, keyboards | Often mixed with additives, so harder to recycle |
| **Glass** | Screens, CRT tubes, tube lights | May contain lead or coatings |
| **Lithium, cobalt, nickel** | Rechargeable batteries | Valuable, but batteries can catch fire if damaged |

The exact mix depends on the product. A refrigerator is mostly steel, plastics and compressor parts, while a phone is packed with small amounts of many different metals.

## Which e waste materials are hazardous?

Some of the substances inside electronics are harmful if released into soil, water or air. Common examples include:

- **Lead:** found in older CRT glass and in some solder
- **Mercury:** found in some lamps and older display backlights
- **Cadmium:** found in some batteries and older components
- **Brominated flame retardants:** found in some plastics and circuit boards
- **Refrigerants and oils:** found in older fridges and air conditioners

These are generally safe while the device is intact. The risk rises when e waste is crushed, burnt or acid-leached in unregulated settings. Smoke from burning cables and boards, and runoff from acid baths, can affect the workers doing the job and the people living nearby.

This is the main reason to avoid handing e waste to anyone who cannot show they are authorised.

## Why is e waste material worth recovering?

Recovering materials from e waste does two things. It keeps hazardous substances out of the environment, and it returns metals and plastics to the supply chain so less has to be mined or produced from scratch.

That is the circular economy idea in practice: keep materials in use for as long as possible. If you want to see how this thinking applies to a business, read [from linear to circular: five shifts every business can start with](/blog/linear-to-circular-five-shifts).

## How is e waste material recycled?

Authorised recyclers follow a controlled process. In simple terms:

1. **Collection and logging:** items are collected, weighed and recorded.
2. **Sorting:** devices are separated by type and condition. Reusable items may be refurbished.
3. **Manual dismantling:** batteries, circuit boards, cables, plastics and glass are removed by hand.
4. **Mechanical processing:** shredding and separation split metals from plastics.
5. **Material recovery:** metals are sent on for refining, and plastics and glass go to appropriate recyclers.
6. **Safe disposal:** residues that cannot be recovered are treated or disposed of as required.

Data-bearing devices need extra care. Back up what you need and wipe or reset phones, laptops and drives before handing them over.

## What should you do with your e waste?

### If you are a household or housing society

- Keep e waste separate from wet and dry waste.
- Store small items such as chargers and batteries in a box until you have enough for a pickup.
- Never burn it, throw it in a drain or hand it to an unverified scrap dealer.
- Book a pickup with an authorised recycler, or organise a collection day. Our [zero-waste drive guide for housing societies](/blog/zero-waste-drive-housing-society-guide) shows how.

### If you are a business

Companies that produce, sell or use large volumes of electronics have obligations under India's E-Waste Management Rules. The specifics depend on your role, whether producer, bulk consumer, refurbisher or recycler. Always check the current text on the website of the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change or the Central Pollution Control Board.

For a plain-language summary, read [what the E-Waste Management Rules ask of your business](/blog/e-waste-rules-india-what-businesses-need-to-know). If you need hands-on help, our [EPR compliance services](/solutions/epr) cover e-waste, plastic packaging and batteries.

## Checklist: before you hand over e waste

- Back up and erase personal or company data
- Remove batteries where possible and store them separately
- Keep items intact; do not break screens or open devices
- Ask the recycler for proof of authorisation
- Ask for a receipt or certificate of disposal, especially for business waste
- Record quantities for your own compliance and sustainability reporting

## Frequently asked questions

### What is e waste material in simple words?
E waste material is the discarded electronics and electrical items, and the parts and substances inside them. It includes things like phones, laptops, appliances, batteries, cables and bulbs.

### What valuable materials can be recovered from e waste?
Common recoverable materials include copper, aluminium, iron and steel, along with small amounts of gold, silver and palladium from circuit boards. Plastics, glass and battery metals such as lithium, cobalt and nickel can also be recovered through proper recycling.

### Is e waste material dangerous?
Intact devices are generally low risk, but some contain substances like lead, mercury and cadmium. The danger comes mainly from crude methods such as open burning or acid extraction. Authorised recyclers use controlled processes to manage these risks.

### Can I sell my old electronics as scrap?
You can, but choose carefully. Working devices may be resold or refurbished. For non-working items, use an authorised e waste recycler rather than an informal dealer who may dismantle them unsafely.

### How do businesses dispose of e waste legally?
Businesses should hand e waste to authorised recyclers or refurbishers and keep records of what was handed over. The exact obligations depend on your category under the E-Waste Management Rules, so check the official rules or speak to a compliance expert.
