Your old phone is in a drawer. The broken laptop is in the store room. The office has a pile of dead keyboards, cables and printers that nobody knows what to do with.

This is **e waste**, and it is one of the fastest-growing waste streams in India. This guide explains what e waste is, why it needs special handling, and exactly how to dispose of it safely, whether you are a household or a business.

## Key takeaways

- **E waste** is any discarded electrical or electronic item, from phones and chargers to servers and printers.
- It should never go in your regular dustbin or to a scrap dealer who burns or dismantles it in the open.
- Households can hand it to authorised collectors or recyclers; businesses have formal responsibilities under India's e-waste rules.
- Safe recycling recovers valuable metals and keeps toxic substances out of soil, water and air.
- Wipe your data before you let go of any device.

## What is e waste?

**E waste** (electronic waste) means electrical and electronic equipment that is no longer wanted, no longer works, or has reached the end of its life. If it has a plug, a battery or a circuit board, it becomes e waste once you discard it.

Common examples:

- Mobile phones, chargers, power banks and earphones
- Laptops, desktops, monitors and keyboards
- Televisions, set-top boxes and routers
- Refrigerators, air conditioners, washing machines and microwaves
- Printers, cartridges, servers and networking equipment
- Batteries, LED bulbs and tube lights

For a deeper look at what is inside these items, read our post on [e waste material and where it goes](/blog/e-waste-material-what-it-contains).

## Why is e waste dangerous if thrown away with regular garbage?

Electronics contain a mix of useful and harmful materials. Valuable metals such as copper, gold and aluminium sit alongside substances like lead, mercury and cadmium, depending on the product.

When e waste is dumped in landfills or burned informally, these materials can leach into soil and water or release toxic fumes. People who dismantle devices by hand without protection are at the greatest risk. Proper recycling avoids this and sends recovered materials back into manufacturing.

This is a good example of a circular approach: the same resources get used again instead of being mined fresh. See our piece on [five shifts from linear to circular](/blog/linear-to-circular-five-shifts) for more.

## How to dispose of e waste safely at home

You do not need a complicated process. Follow these steps.

1. **Decide: reuse, repair or recycle.** A working phone or laptop may be useful to someone else. Donate it or sell it through a trusted channel.
2. **Back up and wipe your data.** Sign out of accounts, remove SIM and memory cards, and do a factory reset. For hard drives with sensitive files, ask the recycler about secure data destruction.
3. **Collect items in one place.** Keep a box for small items like cables, chargers and batteries. Store them dry and away from children.
4. **Don't break open devices.** Damaged lithium batteries can be a fire risk, so keep them intact.
5. **Hand it to an authorised collector or recycler.** Use a doorstep pickup service, a producer take-back programme or a collection drive.
6. **Keep a record.** For larger quantities, ask for a receipt or acknowledgement.

Housing societies can make this easier by organising a collection day. Our [guide to running a zero-waste drive in your housing society](/blog/zero-waste-drive-housing-society-guide) shows how.

## Where can you give away e waste in India?

Your main options are:

| Option | Best for | Things to check |
|---|---|---|
| Doorstep pickup by an authorised recycler | Households and small offices | Ask whether they send material to an authorised facility |
| Producer or brand take-back | Appliances and devices from a specific brand | Terms vary by brand |
| Community collection drives | Housing societies, schools, RWAs | Confirm who is collecting and where it goes |
| Direct handover to a recycler | Businesses with bulk volumes | Ask for documentation |
| Local scrap dealer | Not recommended | Informal dismantling is often unsafe |

Whichever route you choose, ask where the material will end up. A reliable partner will answer clearly.

## What do the e waste rules in India say?

India regulates e waste through the E-Waste (Management) Rules, which follow the principle of **Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)**. In simple terms, manufacturers, producers, refurbishers and recyclers have defined obligations to ensure that electronics are collected and recycled in an environmentally sound way.

Businesses that buy and use electronics also have a role: they should hand over end-of-life equipment only to authorised channels and keep records. Rules and targets are updated from time to time, so always check the latest notifications on the official Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) website.

## How should businesses manage e waste?

Offices, schools and factories generate e waste steadily, and it often piles up because nobody owns the process. A simple system works well.

- **Make an inventory.** Track IT assets and note when they reach end of life.
- **Nominate an owner.** One person or team should be responsible for e waste.
- **Segregate and store safely.** Keep batteries, screens and bulky items separate and dry.
- **Use only authorised recyclers.** Collect certificates or records for your files.
- **Include e waste in sustainability reporting.** Waste data increasingly feeds into ESG disclosures.

## What happens to e waste after collection?

At an authorised facility, devices are sorted, dismantled and processed. Batteries, circuit boards, plastics and metals are separated. Recoverable materials such as copper, aluminium and precious metals are sent back to manufacturers, while hazardous parts are treated in a controlled way.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is e waste in simple words?
E waste is any electronic or electrical product that has been thrown away or has reached the end of its useful life. Old phones, laptops, TVs, chargers and appliances are all examples. It needs special recycling because it contains both valuable and harmful materials.

### Can I throw e waste in the dustbin?
No. Electronic items should not go in your regular wet or dry waste bins. Batteries and screens can be hazardous, and the metals inside are worth recovering. Keep e waste separate and give it to an authorised collector.

### Is old electronic equipment worth money?
Sometimes. Working devices can be resold or donated, and some recyclers or take-back programmes offer value for certain items. Even when you are not paid, safe disposal protects your community and recovers useful resources.

### How do I make sure my data is safe before disposing of a device?
Back up what you need, sign out of all accounts, remove SIM and memory cards, and perform a factory reset. For laptops, desktops and servers holding sensitive data, ask your recycler about certified data destruction before handing them over.

### Who is responsible for e waste in India?
Responsibility is shared. Producers have EPR obligations, recyclers must process material safely, and consumers and businesses should use authorised channels. Details are set out in the E-Waste (Management) Rules, so check the official CPCB website for current requirements.
