Resident Welfare Associations (RWAs) are among the most powerful units of change in an Indian city. A single society can generate a significant amount of waste every day, and a committed group of residents can transform what happens to it. A well-run **zero-waste drive** is often the spark.

Here is a practical playbook, built from what works on the ground.

## Before the drive: plan for after the drive

The most common mistake is treating the drive as the goal. The real goal is the **habit** that continues afterwards. Before choosing a date, agree on:

- **What you want to change.** Source segregation? Composting wet waste? Collecting e-waste or textiles?
- **Who will carry it on.** Name a small core team, ideally with a representative from each tower or lane.
- **Who will take the material.** Line up authorised recyclers and collection partners *before* you collect anything.

## Step 1: Get the basics of segregation right

India's Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 require households to segregate waste at source. A simple three-way split covers most of it:

- **Wet waste**: kitchen scraps, fruit and vegetable peels, tea leaves, garden waste. *Compostable.*
- **Dry waste**: paper, cardboard, plastics, metal, glass, clean packaging. *Recyclable when kept clean.*
- **Domestic hazardous waste**: batteries, bulbs, expired medicines, sanitary waste, paint. *Needs separate handling.*

Make it visual. Colour-coded bins and a one-page guide on every floor do more than any WhatsApp forward.

## Step 2: Choose your drive format

- **Collection drive**: residents bring dry recyclables, e-waste or old clothes to a central point on a set day.
- **Segregation week**: volunteers check bins at collection time and give friendly, door-to-door feedback.
- **Compost kick-off**: set up a community composter or distribute home-composting kits with a demo.
- **Kids' challenge**: children often become the most persistent enforcers at home.

## Step 3: Partner with the right people

- **Authorised recyclers** for dry waste and e-waste, with receipts recording quantities.
- **Your housekeeping staff**, who handle the waste every day. Train them, involve them and recognise them.
- **Your municipal body**, which may provide separate collection vehicles or composting support.

## Step 4: Measure everything

Weigh what you collect, by type. Photograph the setup. Record how many households took part. This data:

- shows residents their impact ("our society diverted X kg from landfill this month"),
- helps the RWA make a case for composters or better bins,
- and turns a one-off event into a **track record**.

## Step 5: Keep the momentum

- **Share results** within a week, while people still remember taking part.
- **Repeat on a rhythm**, for example a monthly collection day, so it becomes routine.
- **Recognise contributors**: the tower with the best segregation, the most consistent volunteers.
- **Give people a reason to keep logging actions.** Platforms like **KarmaVerse** let residents record verified sustainable actions and earn KarmaCoins, keeping the feedback loop going between drives.
